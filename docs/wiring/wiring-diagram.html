Three independent power sources feed this system: the Pi's wall adapter (which also powers the ESC), the Mac's USB-C (which powers the ESP32), and the motor supply. They are only tied together by the common ground rail. That single shared ground is what makes every signal in this diagram meaningful — see page 4.

Why these pins

GPIO 10–13 are the ESP32-S3's native FSPI (SPI2) IOMUX pins, so SPI runs at full speed with no GPIO-matrix routing penalty.

Already confirmed on your hardware (read live over EtherCAT with servo_master eth0 regs): PDI Control = 0x05 (SPI Slave), so the SCS_FUNC pull-up is already fitted on-board. You do not need a 4.7K resistor.
MISO / MOSI are adjacent on the header. Swapping them is the classic mistake — it fails silently, reading 0x00 forever with no other symptom. Drive NSS as a plain GPIO, not hardware CS: the ESC needs it held low across a whole multi-byte transaction.

3.3 V logic into a 5 V board is fine here. The A4988's logic-high threshold is 2.0 V, so the ESP32's 3.3 V STEP/DIR/EN signals register correctly. Feed the board's VDD logic rail 5 V; no level shifter needed.

Setting the A4988 current limit — do this before the motor spins

Turn the trimpot and measure Vref between the pot's metal top and GND with the multimeter, 12 V applied, motor disconnected:

Sense resistor (marked on board)FormulaTarget for 1.0 A
R100 = 0.1 ΩVref = I × 0.80.80 V
R050 = 0.05 ΩVref = I × 0.40.40 V
Fit the 100 µF capacitor across VMOT and GND before first power-up. Inductive spikes from the coils will destroy an A4988 otherwise — this is the single most common way these boards die, and both the Allegro datasheet and Pololu's notes call it out explicitly.

Never unplug the motor while 12 V is on. Same failure mode.

Identifying the coil pairs

Set the multimeter to ohms. Two wires reading about 4.1 Ω are one coil; across different coils reads open. Your 17HS4023's datasheet phase resistance is 4.1 Ω, so this is a definitive check — do not trust wire colour. Pair them into 1A/1B and 2A/2B. If the motor buzzes without turning, swap one pair.

Page 4 — Ground, power sequence, pre-flight

The page that prevents most of the debugging.

Ground topology

This is the wire people forget. The ESP32 is powered from your Mac's USB-C while the ESC runs off the Pi. Two supplies with no shared reference means the SPI signals have no valid return path, and you read garbage. Symptom: SPI returns all 0x00 or all 0xFF, while the wiring looks perfectly correct.
Never feed 5 V into the module from two places. It already takes 5 V from Pi pin 4. Do not also connect the ESP32's 5 V pin to it.

Do not wire the motors until 4a passes. Adding a driver, a 12 V rail, current tuning and new firmware at once turns one unknown into five.

Power-up order

  1. Everything off. Pi shut down and unplugged — GPIO pin 4 stays live after poweroff while the adapter is in.
  2. Connect all grounds first.
  3. Connect signal wires.
  4. Power the Pi → module LEDs come on, eth0 links.
  5. Power the motor supply (12 V / 5 V).
  6. Plug the ESP32 USB-C last.

Power down in reverse — USB-C out first.

The ESP32 goes last because it drives signals into the ESC and the A4988. Driving a pin high on an unpowered chip pushes current through its protection diodes — that is how boards get damaged before any software is even written.
